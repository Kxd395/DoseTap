## Known display and calculation differences

1. **Phone colors currently mean different things across charts.** Interval-versus-sleep uses green for a pair classified within the existing timing window and orange outside it; other charts use colors for groups or providers. These are not treatment-effectiveness or safety grades. The iPad's cyan interval series is a neutral recorded-spacing series, not an equivalent in-window classification.
2. **Night Mode affects the entire phone presentation.** Its red filtering can alter chart colors and reduce already weak contrast. The supplied screenshots show unreadable/dim metric values and a color legend that cannot by itself preserve meaning. Use chart-local labels, shapes and accessible descriptions; repair contrast in the rendered Night Mode path without disabling text scaling.
3. **Nonpositive intervals need explicit exclusion.** At baseline the phone's `exactIntervalMinutes` accepts equal timestamps; the scatter can therefore contain a zero interval. The iPad already requires a positive ordered pair. Preserve raw records and flag review; never replace occurrence with entry time or add a day to manufacture a positive value.
4. **Phone sleep totals and reviewed-night estimates are different measures.** Legacy dashboard selection uses 18:00–12:00 primary-episode grouping; reviewed calculations require explicit boundaries and conflict/nap checks. Do not copy a legacy total under a reviewed-total label.
5. **Historic settings are incomplete.** Reminder timing, planned spacing and applicable historical medication window are separate. Do not color old records as compliant using today's preferences or interpret before-reminder as an early dose.
6. **A saved morning rating may be an untouched default.** Show stored quality as subjective and confirmation-unknown where that provenance is absent. A constant outcome cannot support an association or “best interval.” Do not rewrite old answers to fabricate confirmation.
7. **Calendar medications must stay on their taken date.** A 05:05 morning medication belongs to that calendar day; the 18:00 treatment-date group must not display it as yesterday's administration. Its association with the following sleep is a separate analytic relationship, not a record relink.

## Shared viewing model

- **Overview:** a few qualified summary cards, report freshness, source coverage and review counts; no overall treatment-effectiveness score.
- **Dose timing:** date plot, distribution and table with distinct recorded outcome, spacing, reminder and historical-window status.
- **Sleep:** measured duration/coverage, stages where supported, reviewed windows, gaps/conflicts and primary episode versus total distinctions.
- **Medications & daytime:** calendar administrations, symptom/nap observations and explicit previous/next-sleep relationships.
- **Context comparisons:** selected eligible groups with individual points, median/spread and a matched-record list; retain Unknown as a visible group.
- **Night detail:** last food, doses, provider bands, quick logs, wake evidence and check-ins on separate tracks; occurrence and recording time inspectable.
- **Data & definitions:** collection inventory, field purpose/units/source, missingness, calculation versions and export coverage. Keep a visible chart-specific legend beside every visualization.

Use consistent range/source/cohort controls where applicable, including 14 days and 6 months. A range selection must not imply provider coverage outside the downloaded evidence. Show durations as hours/minutes while retaining exact sortable values. Color needs a label or shape; accessible tables must remain available alongside charts.

## Provider transfer: staged follow-on

1. **Contract first:** versioned Apple Health packet with query start/end/completion time, source phone timezone, fulfilled range, fetch status and original `SleepEvidenceSample` records. Current `.appleHealth`/`.whoop` sections are explicitly unavailable; old reports must stay readable. Retain sample UUID/source/stage and original boundaries; validate conflicting duplicate IDs and timestamp ordering.
2. **Bounded preparation:** begin with an explicitly displayed 30-day provider scope, then expand after payload/performance measurement. Query raw overlapping samples once; do not call `fetchSleepEvidence` over months because its resolver scans samples at each boundary. Prepare before the nearby request's 30-second deadline; context changes/backgrounding must fence late results.
3. **Coherent report:** capture local SQLite after provider preparation and retain both capture times. Encode and check the final payload against the 32 MiB transport limit, including base64 expansion. Never silently truncate samples/sources; expose failure or a smaller scope. No new phone clinical-database import or cloud upload is required.
4. **Shared calculation:** decode exact reviewed-window/dose/nap/source identities and reuse `SleepEvidenceResolution`, `ReviewedWindowAssessment`, `ReviewedNightSleepProjection` and `ReviewedDoseSleepMetrics`. Missing/invalid reviewed windows remain ineligible for those metrics; an automatic reporting period must be labeled separately.
5. **Useful first display:** sleep evidence timeline, coverage/source detail and qualified Dose 2 awakening/return panel. Empty successful Health queries mean no readable observations, not verified permission or no sleep. Unknown gaps are neither awake nor asleep.
6. **WHOOP separately:** transfer scored source summaries and query/recovery status with sleep IDs and completeness masks. Keep aggregate stages/disturbances distinct from Apple interval evidence; do not manufacture a hypnogram or Dose 2 return time from totals. WHOOP recovery failure must not erase available sleep.
